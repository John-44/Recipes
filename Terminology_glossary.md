# Terminology glossary

This file pins the exact words the recipes in this repository use, so that every recipe calls the same thing by the same name. It lists only terms that at least one recipe uses. The exception is the [tag list](#tags), which holds every tag a recipe can choose from.

Read it before you write or edit a recipe, use its terms exactly, and add any new terms when you finish. The tables aren't in plain A to Z order, so read [How the tables are ordered](#how-the-tables-are-ordered) before you add anything. The full rules are in [Terminology](Recipe_template.md#terminology) in the recipe template.

## How the entries work

- **Term** is the exact wording to use: the same words, spelling, hyphens, word order and capitals. Terms are written as they'd appear mid-sentence, so most are lower case. Names such as Thai, Kashmiri and Charlotte keep their capitals, and so do headings, labels, titles, places, tags and website names. Give a term a capital first letter when it starts a line or a sentence.
- **Family** (in some tables) groups similar terms, such as Chopping or Peeling.
- **Short name** (ingredients and recipe titles only) is the shortest form to use once something has been introduced. You can drop words from the term as far as its short name, so "raw king prawns" can become "king prawns" or "prawns", but never "shrimp".
- You can add a word that describes the size or state you need, such as "1 small onion", "a large saucepan" or "frozen raw prawns", unless the result is in the **Don't use** column.
- **Don't use** lists other names for the same thing. Never use them for that thing, even where they're correct English.
- **Notes** explain anything that's easy to confuse with the term, and how to use it.
- `{Curly brackets}` mark a part you fill in, as in the recipe template.
- Units and amounts (g, ml, tbsp, cans and so on) follow the template's [measurement rules](Recipe_template.md#measurements). Section headings, summary box labels (such as **Makes**), the **Tip:** label, storage labels (such as **Fridge**) and credit labels (such as **Original author**) are set by [the template](Recipe_template.md#the-template) itself. None of these are repeated here.

## How the tables are ordered

Every table is ordered so that similar terms sit together and are easy to compare. Just above each table, an **Order** line or a **Families** list says how.

- **Families:** in a table with a **Family** column, the rows are grouped by family, and the families come in the order given above the table. A **Families** list can include families with no terms yet, to show where future terms go.
- **Scales:** within a family, or in a table without families, terms run along a scale wherever there's a natural one, such as least heat to most, finest to coarsest or mildest to hottest. Where there isn't one, they're A to Z, ignoring brackets, quote marks and italics.

**To add a term:**

1. Find the table it belongs in, and read the **Order** line or **Families** list above it.
2. If the table has families, choose the family the term belongs to. If none fits, add a new family to the **Families** list where it fits best, and say how its terms are ordered.
3. Insert the term at its place in that order. Never just add it to the end of a table.

Dishes the recipes mention, from any cuisine, with their English names and their original names.

## Preparation terms

How ingredients are prepared, mostly written after the comma in an ingredient line. Join two with "and", as in "peeled and finely chopped". When the amount is measured after preparing, write the term before the ingredient instead, as in "400 g peeled raw king prawns" or "1 tbsp grated palm sugar".

**Families**, from cleaning and removing parts, through cutting and bruising, to measuring and seasoning:

| Family | What goes in it | Order within the family |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cleaning | Getting dirt off, such as by scrubbing roots | Lightest to most thorough |
| Trimming | Removing parts you don't eat, such as stalks and seeds | Least removed to most |
| Peeling | Removing skins and shells | Least removed to most |
| Chopping | Cutting into pieces with no set shape | Finest to coarsest |
| Slicing | Cutting into slices | Thinnest to thickest |
| Cutting | Cutting into pieces of a set shape, such as wedges or chunks | Thinnest to chunkiest |
| Grating | Grating and zesting | Finest to coarsest |
| Bruising | Crumpling or crushing ingredients to release their flavour, without cutting them | Lightest to heaviest |
| Measuring | How to fill a measuring spoon | Loosest to firmest |
| Seasoning | Seasoning to taste | A to Z |

## Tags

The full list of tags a recipe can choose from, including some that no recipe uses yet. The [tag rules](Recipe_template.md#tags) in the recipe template say how to choose them and how to set them out in a recipe. If no tag fits, add a new one here, following those rules, at the place the order below gives it. The **Allergens** group is the exception: it's a fixed list, so never add to it.

**Order:** the groups stay in this order. Within each group:

- **Course:** Breakfasts, then a meal's courses in the order you'd eat them, then Snacks and Drinks.
- **Type:** A to Z.
- **Main ingredient:** meat from red to white, then Fish, Seafood, other proteins (A to Z), Vegetables, grains, then pasta and noodles (A to Z), as in the [ingredient families](#ingredients).
- **Diet:** free-from tags first (A to Z), then Vegetarian and Vegan, from least strict to most.
- **Allergens:** A to Z.

| Group | Tags |
| --- | --- |
| Course | Breakfasts, Starters, Mains, Sides, Desserts, Snacks, Drinks |
| Type | Breads, Cakes, Curries, Dressings, Marinades, Pastes, Pickles, Pies, Salads, Sauces, Soups, Spice mixes, Stews, Stir-fries, Stocks |
| Main ingredient | Beef, Lamb, Pork, Chicken, Fish, Seafood, Eggs, Pulses, Tofu, Vegetables, Rice, Noodles, Pasta |

Prawns and other shellfish are **Seafood**, not **Fish**.

The **Allergens** tags are the UK's 14 main allergens, as the [Food Standards Agency](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/food-allergy-and-intolerance-advice-for-consumers/food-allergy-and-intolerance-advice-for-consumers) lists them. **Cereals containing gluten** means wheat, rye, barley and oats. **Soya** is the FSA's "soybeans", and **Sulphites** covers sulphur dioxide too. Prawns are **Crustaceans**, and mussels and oysters are **Molluscs**. Coconut isn't on the list.
