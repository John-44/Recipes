# Declined suggestions

Suggestions for this repository that have been considered and turned down. Read this file before you suggest a change to a recipe, the [Recipe template](Recipe_template.md) or the [Terminology glossary](Terminology_glossary.md), and leave out anything it lists unless the user brings it up again.

**To add a suggestion:** put it under the heading for the file it's about, roughly where it would have gone in that file, adding the heading if there isn't one yet. Say in a few words what was suggested, and why it was turned down if a reason was given. Add it in the same pull request as the changes that were accepted. If something listed here is accepted later, delete it in the pull request that makes the change.

## Recipe template

- A "Last cooked as written" date on each recipe.
- A minimum wait, such as 2 hr, before something goes in **Start ahead**. It's for waits of any length.
- Moving waits of 1 hr or more after `plus` in **Total time**. The cut-off is 2 hr.
- **Before you start** bullets for preparation that matters more than usual, such as chopping very finely so a paste isn't gritty, or for measuring everything out before Step 1.
- Saying how to cook each accompaniment in **To serve**, such as the jasmine rice, or linking a rice recipe.
- Rounding liquids that don't fit a metric cup measure to the nearest one, such as 200 ml to ¾ metric cup. They stay in ml.
- Dropping **Credits** on the grounds that git history already records who changed each recipe.

## Terminology glossary

- Changing the **crema** entry so a recipe's description can use the word without explaining it in brackets.
- "Chill" or "chilling" for refrigerating, or loosening the entry that rules them out.
- Changing the example "soaking dried chillies" to "microwaving dried chillies" in the note for **Prep the ingredients**.
- Trimming the step title, note topic, shop, brand, food label or source tables.

## Licence

- A note that the licence doesn't cover the sentence or two of a source's wording that a recipe can use.

## Midnight Spaghetti

- Rewording the description to mention the herbs, to say what the smoked garlic and pine nuts add rather than "umami", and to avoid "at night during an evening".
- Putting the parsley for the sauce and for serving on one ingredient line, as Reverse Seared Ribeye with Pan Sauce does with its coriander, so the amounts in Step 1 match the list.
- A thickness for the garlic slices.
- Boiling the spaghetti for 2 minutes less than the time on the pack, as Simone Acquarelli does in the video, or changing La Molisana's time from 11 minutes to the 10 on its pack.
- Starting Step 3 as soon as the spaghetti goes in, and making it clearer that the heat goes down to low once, after the last of the pasta water.
- Replacing "Don't let the garlic or the oil burn" with what to do if the garlic colours before it softens.
- A fix in Step 4 for a crema that looks oily, and al dente as the test for when the spaghetti is done.
- Keeping back 2 tbsp of the olive oil to stir in uncooked at the end of Step 4.
- A **Taste and adjust** step.
- A buying note for the smoked garlic.
- Telling the cook to check the pine nuts' label on the **Allergens** line, though pine nuts aren't one of the 14.
- Crediting the YouTube source with the name, the late-night custom and the boiling time, and dropping the timestamp from its link.

## Pumpkin Pie

- Saying in **Equipment** that the measuring cup should be metal, and what it's for.
- A homemade shortcrust pastry recipe instead of the store-bought all-butter shortcrust pastry.
- A size and thickness to roll the pastry to in Step 1.
- Keeping the pastry trimmings to patch any cracks in the pastry case before the filling goes in.
- Reusing the Step 2 pie guard in Step 5, or leaving the pie guard out of the blind bake, as Serious Eats says the foil folded over the edge already protects it.
- Cooking the pumpkin with the sugar, cornflour and spices in a saucepan before the evaporated milk and eggs go in, as Preppy Kitchen used to.
- Plainer wording: "most of the sugar" and "the rest of the sugar" in Step 4, and "so the pastry comes out pale and soft rather than crisp" in the **Pie dish** note.
- Cutting the pie with a knife dipped in boiling water and wiped dry between cuts.
- A buying note saying most packs of all-butter shortcrust pastry hold less than 350 g. The pastry is now 320 g instead.
- Homemade pumpkin or butternut squash purée as a fallback in the **Pumpkin purée** note.

## Reverse Seared Ribeye with Pan Sauce

- Naming the vegetables the description says the sauce can go over, or dropping "the", and adding "is" to "then finished with butter".
- Salting the steak the night before and leaving it uncovered on the wire rack in the fridge, as an option in **Before you start**.
- A **Before you start** warning that the cast iron skillet's handle gets as hot as the pan, and turning on the extractor fan before heating the ghee in Step 3.
- Mixing the mustard powder with cold water in Step 2, so it has more heat and no lumps.
- Cooking only the coriander stalks in the sauce, and stirring the finely chopped leaves in with the butter.
- Searing for 45–60 seconds a side, as Serious Eats does with the same oven temperatures. The sear stays at 90 seconds a side, and 46°C and 40°C are for medium and medium-rare.
- Making the sauce in a saucepan while the steak is in the oven, then deglazing the cast iron skillet with it after searing and serving the steak straight away.
- Pouring off all but about 1 tbsp of the fat before frying the shallots. The recipe uses 2 tbsp of ghee, and keeps all of it.
- Keeping the heat on high for the whole of Step 6, rather than turning it down to medium as the sauce thickens.
- A **Ribeye steak** note recommending a steak at least 4 cm thick, shared between 2. The steak is 3.5 cm thick.
- Naming example wines, such as Côtes du Rhône, Merlot or Grenache, in the **Red wine** note.
- Rewording the **Origin** to say several cooks seem to have developed the reverse sear independently, J. Kenji López-Alt among them, instead of "test cooks like J. Kenji López-Alt".

## Thai Yellow Curry Paste

- Flagging the 35 min total time as relying on preparation done while the chillies soak.
- "Peeled" on the shallot and garlic lines.
- Advice to slice the lemongrass and chop the galangal as finely as you can.
- Tasting the soaked chillies, and using fewer if they're hot.
- Washing the jar because the fresh turmeric stains it.
- A buying note for the curry powder, such as recommending S&B or any other mild one.
- A substitute for galangal, such as extra ginger and the zest of 1 lime.
- A number of bird's eye chillies in **More heat**.
- Roasting the shallots and garlic. They stay raw, and the **Roasted shallots and garlic** note explains the difference.
- A mortar-and-pestle note.
- Celery in the warning about the curry powder on the **Allergens** line.

## Thai Yellow Prawn Curry

- Taking the time spent toasting the crispy fried shallots out of the cook time.
- A buying note for whole head-on prawns.
- Naming a brand of store-bought paste, such as Mae Ploy or Aroy-D.
- Prawn-shell stock instead of the low-salt chicken stock, or "or water" after it.
- Cherry tomatoes.
- Leaving out the kaffir lime leaves. They stay, even though Hot Thai Kitchen says *gaeng garee* doesn't use them.
- "Makrut" instead of "kaffir".
- Tamarind water made from a block, or advice to avoid Indian tamarind concentrate.
- A gram weight for the palm sugar.
- *Ajat* (cucumber relish) or roti as sides.
- Homemade crispy fried shallots.
- Butterflying the prawns, or cutting deeper along the back when deveining them.
- A thickness target, or a fix for too much salt, in **Taste and adjust**.
- A tip on why to toast the crispy fried shallots.
- Moving the crispy fried shallots out of the pan once they're toasted.
- In **Storage and make-ahead**: counting the fridge's 2 days from when the make-ahead base was made, saying to cool it within 1–2 hours, making the prawns steaming hot when reheating, or a limit on how long fresh or thawed prawns keep before cooking.
