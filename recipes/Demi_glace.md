# 🫗 Demi-Glace

A deep, glossy, intensely savoury sauce base, in the modern restaurant style. Unroasted beef bones simmer for a day for their gelatine, then deeply roasted chicken carcasses join them for flavour. The stock is fortified with seared vegetables, roasted meat trim and a pinçage, then reduced until thick and finished with infused herbs, soy sauce, sherry vinegar and honey. Unlike classic demi-glace, it isn't thickened with flour, which mutes the flavour.

| Prep time | Cook time | Total time | Makes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 min | 6 hr, plus 30 hr simmering the stock | 7 hr, plus 30 hr simmering the stock | About 1 L (12 pucks of about 80 ml) |

## 📌 Before you start

- Ask a butcher for the beef bones and chicken carcasses (see **Beef bones**).
- The beef bones simmer for 24 hours, so the hob stays on overnight. Check that your hob won't switch itself off after a few hours, as some induction hobs do.

## 🔪 Equipment

- Pot holding at least 12 L, with a lid
- Ladle
- 2 large roasting tins
- Tongs
- Chinois (or a fine sieve)
- Heatproof bowl holding at least 6 L (or a second pot)
- Measuring cup
- Kitchen string
- 12-hole silicone muffin tin, for freezing
- Ice cube tray, for freezing smaller portions

## 🥕 Ingredients

#### For the stock

- 2 kg beef bones, cut into 5–8 cm chunks
- 6 L cold water
- 2.5 kg chicken carcasses
- 2 onions, cut into thick wedges
- 2 large carrots, cut into 5 cm chunks
- 3 celery sticks, cut into 5 cm chunks
- 250 g chestnut mushrooms (optional)

#### For the demi-glace

- 500 g meat trim
- 2 tbsp vegetable oil
- 2 onions, finely sliced
- 2 large carrots, peeled and finely sliced
- 2 celery sticks, finely sliced
- 3 tbsp tomato purée
- 2 metric cups red wine
- 4 bay leaves
- 6 thyme sprigs
- 10 g flat-leaf parsley stalks
- 1 celery heart
- 1 tbsp soy sauce, plus extra to taste
- 1 tsp sherry vinegar, plus extra to taste
- 1 tsp honey, plus extra to taste
- ½ tsp freshly ground black pepper, plus extra to taste

## 🍳 Directions

**For the stock**

#### Step 1: Simmer the beef bones.

Put the beef bones and the cold water in the pot, and bring to a simmer over medium-high heat, 30–45 minutes. As it heats, skim off the grey foam that rises to the top with the ladle.

Turn the heat down to low, so it stays at a gentle simmer, and simmer with the lid ajar for 24 hours. Skim off any foam and fat every few hours, and add boiling water whenever the bones start to poke out, including last thing before you go to bed.

> **Tip:** Unroasted bones give the most gelatine, which is what makes demi-glace thick and glossy. Any part of a bone above the water stops giving it up.

#### Step 2: Roast the chicken carcasses.

About 1¼ hours before the bones have simmered for 24 hours, preheat the oven to 220°C (200°C fan).

Press the chicken carcasses flat and spread them out in a single layer in the 2 roasting tins. Roast them until deep brown all over, about 1 hour, turning them over halfway. Stop before they blacken.

While they roast, cut the onions for the stock into thick wedges, and the carrots and celery into 5 cm chunks.

> **Tip:** Deep roasting gives the chicken its flavour and colour, but it damages gelatine, and chicken gives up its flavour far faster than beef bones give up their gelatine. That's why the beef bones go in unroasted, and simmer on their own first.

#### Step 3: Simmer the stock.

When the bones have simmered for 24 hours, lift the carcasses into the pot with tongs, leaving their fat behind. Pour the fat out of the roasting tins, then ladle ½ metric cup of the stock into each tin and scrape up the fond (the browned bits stuck to the bottom of the tin). Pour it into the pot.

Add the onions, carrots, celery and mushrooms, and add boiling water if needed so everything is just covered. Bring back to a simmer over medium-high heat, then turn the heat down to low and simmer gently, with the lid ajar, for 6 hours, skimming off any foam and fat every hour or so.

While it simmers, finely slice the onions and celery for the demi-glace, and peel and finely slice the carrots.

#### Step 4: Strain the stock.

Lift the bones and vegetables out of the pot with tongs. Ladle the stock through the chinois into the heatproof bowl, then tip the pot to pour in the last of it.

Leave the stock to settle for 5 minutes, then skim the fat off the top with the ladle. You'll have about 5 L. Wash the pot and the chinois.

**For the demi-glace**

#### Step 5: Roast the meat trim.

Preheat the oven to 220°C (200°C fan). Spread the meat trim out in a single layer in a clean roasting tin, and roast until deep brown, 40–50 minutes, turning it over halfway. While it roasts, start Step 6.

#### Step 6: Sear the vegetables.

Heat the oil in the pot over high heat until it starts to smoke. Add the onions, carrots and celery for the demi-glace, and sear them, stirring occasionally, until they're deep brown at the edges, 15–20 minutes. If they start to blacken, turn the heat down to medium-high.

#### Step 7: Make the pinçage.

Make a pinçage (brown the tomato purée, then deglaze it with wine, several times over):

- Turn the heat down to medium. Stir in the tomato purée and fry it with the vegetables, stirring often, until it turns from bright red to a darker brick or rust colour and starts to catch on the bottom of the pot, 3–5 minutes. Stop before it blackens.
- Pour in ½ metric cup of the wine to deglaze the pot, scraping up the fond. Cook, stirring often, until the wine has evaporated and the tomato purée starts to catch on the bottom of the pot again, 3–5 minutes.
- Repeat 3 more times with the rest of the wine, ½ metric cup at a time, until it smells toasty, not tomatoey.

> **Tip:** Browning the tomato purée in rounds takes away its raw, sharp taste. Deglazing each time it catches stops it burning, which would make the demi-glace bitter.

#### Step 8: Reduce the demi-glace.

Lift the meat trim into the pot with tongs, leaving its fat in the roasting tin. Pour the fat out of the tin, then ladle ½ metric cup of the stock into it, scrape up the fond and pour it into the pot. Add the rest of the stock.

Bring to a simmer over high heat, then turn the heat down to medium so it keeps simmering. Cook, uncovered, skimming off the foam every 10–15 minutes, until it coats the back of a spoon (when you draw a finger through it, the line stays clear), 3–4 hours. You'll have about 1.2 L. As it thickens, stir it more often so it doesn't catch on the bottom of the pot.

> **Tip:** The foam is fat and protein. Skimming it off often keeps the demi-glace clear and clean-tasting.

#### Step 9: Strain the demi-glace.

Pour the demi-glace through the chinois into a saucepan. Don't press the solids, or bits will be pushed through.

#### Step 10: Infuse the herbs.

Tuck the bay leaves, thyme sprigs and parsley stalks in among the stalks of the celery heart, and tie them into a bundle with kitchen string. Drop the bundle into the hot demi-glace and leave it to infuse off the heat, with the lid on, for 15 minutes, then lift it out.

> **Tip:** The pale yellow heart of the celery adds a subtle, fresh note to the deep flavour of the demi-glace. The darker green sticks would taste too strongly of celery.

#### Step 11: Taste and adjust.

Stir in the soy sauce, sherry vinegar, honey and pepper, then taste. It should be rich, deeply savoury and meaty, with a hint of sweetness and a gentle tang. Sauces made with it are often reduced further, which concentrates the salt, so season it lightly.

Adjust it ½ tsp at a time: more soy sauce if it needs salt, more sherry vinegar if it tastes flat or heavy, more honey if it tastes sharp, or more pepper if it needs warmth.

#### Step 12: Use or store.

Use what you need straight away, such as in Chilli con Carne or Reverse Seared Ribeye with Pan Sauce. Freeze the rest.

## 📝 Notes and substitutions

- **Beef bones:** Ask for knuckle or other joint bones, cut into 5–8 cm chunks. They're full of cartilage, which gives the most gelatine.
- **Celery:** A head of celery gives you the 5 celery sticks and the celery heart. Use the darker green outer sticks for the stock and the demi-glace, and keep the pale yellow heart for Step 10.
- **Red wine:** Use an inexpensive, medium-bodied red. Avoid oaky, sweet or very tannic (mouth-drying) wines, because reducing the wine concentrates those flavours.

## 🫙 Storage and make-ahead

- 🧊 **Fridge:** Up to 2 days in an airtight container. Refrigerate it within 1–2 hours. It sets to a firm jelly when cold.
- ❄️ **Freezer:** Up to 3 months in a freezer bag, or up to 1 year if vacuum packed. Pour it into the silicone muffin tin so it cools quickly, and within 1–2 hours, freeze it uncovered until firm. For smaller frozen portions, use an ice cube tray instead. Pop out the frozen pucks or cubes, then vacuum pack them or put them in a freezer bag. Add them straight from frozen to a simmering sauce, or thaw them in the fridge and use them within 24 hours.
- 📅 **Make ahead:** Make the stock up to 2 days ahead, up to the end of Step 4. Divide it between airtight containers so it cools quickly, and refrigerate it within 1–2 hours. The fat sets on top, so it's easy to lift off before you carry on from Step 5.
