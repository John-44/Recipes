# 🍲 Viennese Beef Goulash

A rich, deep red goulash of beef shin, slow cooked with as much onion as beef until the onions melt into a thick, smooth gravy. Known in Vienna as *Wiener Saftgulasch*, it's flavoured with plenty of Hungarian sweet paprika, dried marjoram, garlic and a little lemon zest, with the roasted shin bone and tomato purée browned with red wine for extra depth. Like most stews, it tastes even better the next day. Serve with mashed or boiled and buttered potatoes, or *Spätzle* for a more traditional experience.

| Prep time | Cook time | Total time | Servings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 45 min | 6 hr | 6 hr 45 min, plus overnight in the fridge | 10 |

## 📌 Before you start

- Ask a butcher for a Thor's hammer beef shin, and ask them to saw it into 2 or 3 pieces, so the bone fits in the Dutch oven (see **Thor's hammer beef shin**).
- Buy yellow onions and Hungarian sweet paprika (see **Yellow onions** and **Hungarian sweet paprika**).
- At least 1 day, or ideally 2 days, before you serve it, make the goulash up to the end of Step 12, so it can rest in the fridge.

## 🔪 Equipment

- Roasting tin
- Dutch oven holding at least 6 L
- Tongs
- Measuring cup
- Garlic crusher

## 🥕 Ingredients

- 1 Thor's hammer beef shin (about 3 kg), meat cut off the bone and cut into 4 cm chunks (about 2 kg once prepared)
- 2 kg yellow onions, peeled and finely sliced into half-moons
- 8 garlic cloves, peeled
- 110 g ghee (about 8 tbsp) (used in Steps 3 and 5)
- 2 tbsp tomato purée
- 1½ metric cups red wine (used in Steps 4 and 7)
- 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar
- 2 tbsp dried marjoram
- 2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 110 g Hungarian sweet paprika
- 450 ml beef stock
- 2 tsp smoked sea salt flakes
- 1½ tsp MSG
- 4 bay leaves
- 1 lemon, for its zest
- 2 tbsp plain flour (only if needed, see Step 11)

## 🍳 Directions

#### Step 1: Prep the ingredients.

- Preheat the oven to 220°C (200°C fan).
- Cut the meat off the bone, then cut it into 4 cm chunks, trimming off only any thick fat on the outside. Keep the bone for Step 2.
- Peel the onions, cut them in half lengthways (from the stalk end to the base), then finely slice them into half-moons, so they all cook evenly.
- Peel the garlic.

> **Tip:** The fat left on the beef melts into the sauce, and the connective tissue keeps the beef juicy as it cooks.

#### Step 2: Roast the bone.

Put the pieces of bone in a roasting tin and roast them until deep brown, 45–60 minutes, turning them over halfway. While they roast, start Step 3.

When the bone is deep brown, take it out of the oven and set it aside for Step 10. Turn the oven down to 140°C (120°C fan).

#### Step 3: Sear the beef.

Put 1 tbsp of the ghee in the Dutch oven, and heat it over high heat until it starts to smoke.

Sear the beef in 4 batches of about 500 g, turning the pieces occasionally with tongs, until deep brown on all sides, 6–8 minutes. Lift each batch into a bowl and set it aside. Before each of the next 3 batches, add another 1 tbsp of the ghee and heat it until it starts to smoke.

If the fat smokes heavily or the fond (the browned bits stuck to the bottom of the pan) starts to blacken, turn the heat down to medium-high.

> **Tip:** If the Dutch oven is crowded, the beef releases its juices and stews in them instead of browning.

#### Step 4: Make the pinçage.

Make a pinçage (brown the tomato purée, then deglaze it with wine, several times over) in the fat left in the Dutch oven:

- Turn the heat down to medium-low and wait 1 minute, so the tomato purée doesn't burn in the hot Dutch oven. Add the tomato purée, and fry, stirring all the time, until it turns from bright red to a darker brick or rust colour and starts to catch on the bottom of the Dutch oven, 2–5 minutes. Stop before it blackens.
- Pour in ¼ metric cup of the wine, scraping up the fond. Cook, stirring all the time, until the wine has evaporated and the tomato purée starts to catch on the bottom of the Dutch oven again, 2–4 minutes.
- Repeat 2 more times, ¼ metric cup of the wine at a time, until it smells toasty, not tomatoey.
- Pour in another ¼ metric cup of the wine, and stir, scraping the bottom of the Dutch oven, until nothing is stuck to it. Pour the pinçage into a bowl and set it aside for Step 9.

> **Tip:** Browning the tomato purée in rounds takes away its raw, sharp taste. Deglazing each time it catches stops it burning, which would make the goulash bitter.

#### Step 5: Caramelise the onions.

Put the rest of the ghee (about 4 tbsp) in the Dutch oven over high heat. Once it has melted, add the onions and cook, stirring all the time, for 10 minutes.

Turn the heat down to medium-low and cook, stirring often, until the onions are soft and golden, but not brown, about 25 minutes more. If the bottom of the Dutch oven gets too dry, or a dark crust starts to form on it too fast, add 2 tbsp water and scrape up the browned bits.

Turn the heat down to low, and pour in the cider vinegar to deglaze the onions.

> **Tip:** Well-fried onions are what make the gravy thick and smooth, as they melt into it over the long cook. If they burn, the whole goulash turns bitter.

#### Step 6: Bloom the spices.

Still over low heat, add the dried marjoram and pepper, and bloom them (fry them briefly in the fat, so their flavour passes into it), stirring all the time, until fragrant, 30–60 seconds. Take care not to burn them.

> **Tip:** Many of the flavours in dried herbs and spices dissolve only in fat. Frying them in it draws those flavours out and spreads them through the whole goulash.

#### Step 7: Bloom the paprika.

Take the Dutch oven off the heat and leave it to cool for 1 minute. Still off the heat, add the paprika and stir vigorously for 60 seconds.

Then immediately pour in the rest of the wine (½ metric cup) to deglaze the Dutch oven, so the paprika doesn't burn and turn bitter. Stir, scraping the bottom, until no paprika is stuck to it.

> **Tip:** It's very easy to burn the paprika, so care must be taken to ensure the Dutch oven is not too hot. But if it's too cold, the paprika won't bloom, so getting the temperature right is a fine line.

#### Step 8: Dissolve the seasonings.

Stir the smoked sea salt flakes and MSG into the stock in a jug until they dissolve.

#### Step 9: Reduce the sauce.

Put the Dutch oven back over medium-low heat. Add the pinçage, the stock and the bay leaves, then crush the garlic into the Dutch oven with the garlic crusher. Grate in the zest of the lemon, just the yellow part, not the bitter white pith.

Cook, stirring all the time, until most of the liquid has evaporated, about 15 minutes.

#### Step 10: Slow cook the goulash.

Stir in the beef, with any juices from the bowl, and push the roasted pieces of bone down into it. Put the lid on, and cook in the oven at 140°C (120°C fan) for 2 hours.

Turn the oven down to 110°C (90°C fan), and cook for another 1½ hours, stirring every 15 minutes so it doesn't catch on the bottom of the Dutch oven, until the beef is tender enough to break apart with a spoon. If the beef isn't tender yet but the sauce is already thick enough to coat the back of a spoon (when you draw a finger through it, the line stays clear), stir in water, ¼ metric cup at a time.

#### Step 11: Dust the goulash.

Skip this step if there's no layer of fat on the surface.

Otherwise, take the Dutch oven out of the oven and dust the layer of fat with the flour. Carefully stir the goulash, then put the lid back on and cook it in the oven for another 15 minutes.

> **Tip:** The flour soaks up the fat before it meets the gravy, so it thickens the goulash without forming lumps.

#### Step 12: Refrigerate the goulash.

Lift out the bay leaves and the pieces of bone. Divide the goulash between airtight containers so it cools quickly, and refrigerate it within 1–2 hours. Leave it in the fridge overnight, or for up to 2 days.

> **Tip:** The sitting time thickens the gravy, tenderises the beef and enhances the flavours even further.

#### Step 13: Serve the goulash.

Reheat as much of the goulash as you'll eat in the Dutch oven or a saucepan over medium-low heat, with the lid on, stirring often, until steaming hot all the way through, about 30 minutes for the whole batch. Reheat it only once. If it's too thick, stir in water, 2 tbsp at a time.

Spoon it into bowls to serve.

## 📝 Notes and substitutions

- **Thor's hammer beef shin:** If you can't find one, use 4 kg bone-in beef short ribs instead, which give about 2 kg of meat and 2 kg of bones. Roast the bones in Step 2, and add them in Step 10, as for the shin bone.
- **Yellow onions:** Use yellow onions, not ordinary onions, which are often sold as "brown onions" or "cooking onions".
- **Red wine:** Use an inexpensive, medium-bodied red. Avoid oaky, sweet or very tannic (mouth-drying) wines, because reducing the wine concentrates those flavours.
- **Caraway seeds:** This recipe explicitly excludes caraway seeds, though most Viennese recipes add them.
- **Hungarian sweet paprika:** It's important to use Hungarian sweet paprika. Don't use smoked paprika. Packs may call it noble sweet.

## 🫙 Storage and make-ahead

- 🧊 **Fridge:** Up to 2 days. Divide it between airtight containers so it cools quickly, and refrigerate it within 1–2 hours.
- ❄️ **Freezer:** Up to 3 months in a freezer bag, or up to 1 year if vacuum packed. Thaw it in the fridge overnight, and eat it within 24 hours.
- ♨️ **Reheat:** Reheat only as much as you'll eat, and only once, in a saucepan over medium-low heat, stirring often, until steaming hot all the way through. If it's too thick, stir in water, 2 tbsp at a time.

## 🗺️ Origin

- **Country:** 🇦🇹 Austria
- **Region:** Vienna

Goulash comes from Hungary, but found its way into Viennese cooking in the early 19th century, where it grew into many versions of its own. *Wiener Saftgulasch* is the best loved of them in Vienna.
