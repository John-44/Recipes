# 🍃 Thai Green Curry Paste

A fresh, fragrant curry paste made from green chillies, lemongrass, galangal, kaffir lime leaves and fresh herbs. In Thailand it's called *prik gaeng khiao waan*, and it's the base of Thai green curry. Green Thai spur chillies add chilli flavour without much heat, and Thai basil deepens the green colour. This batch makes enough for 3 curries, so freeze two-thirds for next time.

| Prep time | Cook time | Total time | Makes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 45 min | 5 min | 50 min | About 550 g (enough for 3 curries for 6) |

## 📌 Before you start

- Green bird's eye chillies and green Thai spur chillies can burn your skin and eyes, so wear gloves to prepare them.

## 🔪 Equipment

- High-speed blender with a full-size jar, such as a Blendtec (or a stick blender and a tall, narrow jug)
- Electric spice grinder
- Gloves, for the chillies

## 🥕 Ingredients

- 12 green bird's eye chillies, stalks removed
- 45 g green Thai spur chillies (*prik chee fah khiao*), stalks removed (about 3)
- 1 tbsp coriander seeds
- 1 tbsp cumin seeds
- 1 tsp white peppercorns
- 1 tsp coarse sea salt
- 1 tsp MSG
- 150 g shallots, roughly chopped (about 6)
- 12 garlic cloves, roughly chopped
- 1½ tbsp Thai fermented shrimp paste (*kapi* or *gapi*)
- 90 g coriander leaves and stalks, washed and roughly chopped
- 15 g Thai basil leaves
- 6 lemongrass stalks, top half and tough outer layers removed, finely sliced (about 60 g once prepared)
- 60 g galangal, peeled and finely chopped (about a 15 cm piece)
- 24 kaffir lime leaves, centre ribs removed, finely sliced
- 20 g coriander roots, scrubbed and finely chopped (about 6)

## 🍳 Directions

#### Step 1: Prep the chillies.

- Split the spur chillies open lengthways, then scrape out the seeds and pith.
- Roughly chop the spur chillies and the bird's eye chillies.

> **Tip:** The spur chillies are there for their chilli flavour and green colour, and most of their heat is in the seeds and pith. The bird's eye chillies bring the heat.

#### Step 2: Toast the spices.

Toast the coriander seeds in a dry frying pan over medium-low heat for 1 minute, shaking the pan. Add the cumin seeds and white peppercorns and keep shaking the pan until they're all fragrant and darken a shade, 1–2 minutes more. Tip them into a small bowl straight away and leave to cool for 2 minutes.

> **Tip:** The spices keep cooking in the hot pan, even off the heat, and burnt spices make the paste bitter.

#### Step 3: Grind the spices.

Put the toasted spices in the spice grinder and grind to a fine powder. Tip the ground spices back into the small bowl, ready for Step 5.

#### Step 4: Blend the paste.

Put the salt, MSG, chillies, shallots, garlic, shrimp paste, coriander leaves and stalks and Thai basil in the high-speed blender, then add the lemongrass, galangal, lime leaves and coriander roots on top.

Blend on the highest speed in short bursts, stopping to scrape down the sides, until the paste has no perceptible woody fibres or gritty particles.

If the jar feels warm when you stop, leave it to cool before blending again. Try not to add extra water. If the paste stops moving, add water 1 tsp at a time, just until it moves again.

> **Tip:** Thai curry recipes expect a thick paste. Starting with the soft, wet ingredients gets the paste moving, but with no liquid in the recipe, a full-size jar usually needs a little water too.

#### Step 5: Blend in the spices.

Add the ground spices to the high-speed blender and blend in short bursts, stopping to scrape down the sides, until very smooth, leaving the jar to cool whenever it feels warm. Rub a little between your fingers: it shouldn't feel gritty.

#### Step 6: Use or store.

Use a third of the paste (about 180 g) for a curry that serves 6, such as [Thai Green Chicken Curry](Thai_green_chicken_curry.md). Freeze the rest.

## 📝 Notes and substitutions

- **Spur chillies:** Look for them in Asian supermarkets. If you can't find any, use the same weight of jalapeño chillies.
- **Ground spices:** Instead of toasting and grinding whole spices, use 1 tbsp ground coriander, 1 tbsp ground cumin and 1 tsp ground white pepper. Skip Steps 2 and 3, and add them in Step 5.
- **Shrimp paste:** Look for it in Asian supermarkets, where it's sold in tubs. The same amount of crumbled dried shrimp paste (*belacan*), which is sold in hard blocks, works too.
- **Thai basil:** Look for it in Asian supermarkets. It's only there for colour, as its flavour cooks away when you fry the paste, so the same weight of spinach leaves works too.
- **Galangal:** Look for it fresh or frozen in Asian supermarkets.
- **Kaffir lime leaves:** Look for them fresh or frozen in Asian supermarkets. Dried ones have much less flavour.
- **Coriander roots:** Look for them in Asian supermarkets. If you can't find any, use the same weight of tender coriander stalks.
- **More heat:** Use more bird's eye chillies, or leave the seeds and pith in the spur chillies.
- **Vegetarian:** Leave out the shrimp paste, or use 1½ tbsp white miso instead.
- **Stick blender:** For Steps 4 and 5, put the lemongrass, galangal, lime leaves and coriander roots in the tall, narrow jug first, then everything else, and lift and move the stick blender around as you blend. If it doesn't all fit, blend it in 2 batches, with half the ground spices in each.

## 🫙 Storage and make-ahead

- 🧊 **Fridge:** Up to 2 days in an airtight container, with cling film pressed onto the surface so it doesn't dry out.
- ❄️ **Freezer:** Up to 3 months in a freezer bag, or up to 1 year if vacuum packed. The paste can be fried straight from frozen, so no need to thaw.

## 🗺️ Origin

- **Country:** 🇹🇭 Thailand
- **Region:** Central Thailand

Green curry is a fairly recent Thai dish. It probably began in the kitchens of the Thai aristocracy in the 1920s, when cooks started making curry paste with fresh green chillies instead of dried red ones.
