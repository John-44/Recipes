# Reverse Seared Ribeye with Pan Sauce

A thick ribeye steak, reverse seared: cooked slowly in a low oven until it's evenly pink from edge to edge, then seared in a very hot pan for a deep brown crust. The sauce is made in the same pan, from the browned bits the steak leaves behind: red wine reduced with shallots, garlic, coriander leaves and stalks, English mustard powder, demi-glace and balsamic vinegar, then finished with butter until glossy. It's rich and peppery, with a sweet-sour tang, and there's plenty of it, so serve it with vegetables too.

| Prep time | Cook time | Total time | Servings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 15 min | 55 min | 1 hr 5 min | 1 |

## Equipment

- Ovenproof meat thermometer
- Wire rack set in a baking tray
- Cast iron skillet at least 24 cm across (or a heavy stainless-steel frying pan)
- Tongs

## Ingredients

- 1 ribeye steak, 3 cm thick (about 400 g)
- ¾ tsp coarse sea salt, plus extra to taste
- 1½ tsp freshly ground black pepper (used in Steps 1 and 5)
- 2 shallots (or 1 banana shallot)
- 8 garlic cloves
- 30 g coriander leaves and stalks (used in Steps 5 and 9)
- 2 tbsp ghee
- 250 ml red wine
- 2 tsp English mustard powder
- 2 tsp thick demi-glace (or concentrated beef stock, see Notes)
- 1 tbsp balsamic vinegar, plus extra to taste
- 2 tsp cold unsalted butter, plus extra to taste

## Directions

#### Step 1: Cook the steak in the oven.

Pat the steak dry with kitchen roll, then season it all over, including the edges, with the salt and ½ tsp of the pepper.

Push the ovenproof meat thermometer into one edge of the steak and along its length, until the tip is in the middle of the thickest part, away from any fat. Put the steak on a wire rack set in a baking tray, and put it in the cold oven.

Turn the oven on to 120°C fan, and cook until the steak reaches 46°C for medium-rare or 40°C for rare, 30–40 minutes.

> **Tip:** The steak keeps cooking as you sear and rest it, so it comes out of the oven well below the temperature you want to eat it at.

#### Step 2: Prep the vegetables.

While the steak is in the oven, peel and finely chop the shallots and the garlic, keeping them separate.

Wash the coriander leaves and stalks, and pat them dry with kitchen roll. Pick off 5 g of the coriander leaves (about a small handful) and roughly chop them, to scatter over in Step 9. Finely chop the rest of the coriander leaves and stalks.

#### Step 3: Sear the steak.

When the steak reaches 41°C (35°C for rare), put the ghee in a cast iron skillet at least 24 cm across, and heat it over high heat until it starts to smoke, 3–5 minutes.

As soon as the steak reaches 46°C (40°C for rare), take it out of the oven, remove the ovenproof meat thermometer, and lay the steak in the hot ghee. Sear it until deep brown, 90 seconds a side, then hold it on its edges with tongs and sear each edge for 30 seconds. Put the steak on a plate to rest while you make the sauce.

> **Tip:** Searing every side, edges included, kills any bacteria on the surface. That's why a whole steak is safe to serve pink in the middle.

#### Step 4: Fry the shallots and garlic.

Take the cast iron skillet off the heat and leave it to cool for 60 seconds. Add the shallots and fry them in the hot fat for 90 seconds, still off the heat, stirring all the time and scraping up the fond (the browned bits stuck to the bottom of the pan).

Put the cast iron skillet back over medium heat, add the garlic and fry, stirring all the time, until fragrant, 90 seconds.

> **Tip:** Cast iron stays very hot after searing. The minute off the heat stops the shallots burning, while leaving them enough heat to sizzle and loosen the fond.

#### Step 5: Add the wine and flavourings.

Pour in the wine, then add the finely chopped coriander leaves and stalks, mustard powder, the rest of the pepper (1 tsp), the demi-glace and the balsamic vinegar. Stir until there are no lumps of mustard powder.

#### Step 6: Reduce the sauce.

Boil hard over high heat, stirring all the time and scraping the bottom of the cast iron skillet to lift any fond that's left, until the sauce is thick enough to coat the back of a spoon, about 4 minutes. As it thickens, gradually turn the heat down to medium, so it doesn't burn on the bottom.

#### Step 7: Stir in the butter.

Take the cast iron skillet off the heat. Add the butter and stir it in, scraping up the last of the fond as it melts, until the sauce is glossy, about 1 minute.

> **Tip:** Off the heat, the butter melts into the sauce and makes it glossy. If the sauce gets too hot once the butter is in, its fat comes out and floats on top in a greasy layer.

#### Step 8: Taste and adjust.

Taste the sauce. It should be rich and savoury, with a kick from the mustard powder and pepper and a sweet-sour tang from the balsamic vinegar, and thick enough to coat the back of a spoon.

Adjust it: a pinch of salt at a time if it tastes flat, ½ tsp of balsamic vinegar at a time if it needs more tang, or 1 tsp of butter at a time if it tastes too sharp. If it's too thick, stir in water 1 tbsp at a time.

#### Step 9: Serve the steak.

Cut the steak across the grain (at right angles to the lines of muscle running through it) into 1 cm slices. Pour some of the sauce over the steak, scatter with the roughly chopped coriander leaves, and serve the rest of the sauce on the side.

## Notes and substitutions

- **Red wine:** Use an inexpensive, medium-bodied red. Avoid oaky, sweet or very tannic (mouth-drying) wines, because reducing the wine concentrates those flavours.
- **English mustard powder:** This recipe uses Colman's, sold in small yellow tins.
- **Demi-glace:** A thick, rich sauce base made by reducing beef or veal stock. If you can't find it, use the same amount of concentrated beef stock, such as [M&S Concentrated Beef Stock](https://www.marksandspencer.com/food/concentrated-beef-stock/p/fdp20002097).
- **Non-stick frying pan:** Don't use one. It shouldn't be heated this hot, and less fond sticks to it for the sauce.

## Storage and make-ahead

- **Fridge:** Up to 2 days, with the steak and sauce in separate airtight containers.
- **Reheat:** Reheat the sauce in a small saucepan over medium-low heat, stirring often, until steaming hot all the way through. Eat the steak cold, as reheating it until steaming hot all the way through would overcook it.

## Origin

- **Country:** United States

The reverse sear technique was independently developed around 2006 by test cooks like J. Kenji López-Alt at Cook's Illustrated as an accessible alternative to expensive sous-vide machines.
