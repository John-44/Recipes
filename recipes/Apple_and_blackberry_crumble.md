# 🍏 Apple and Blackberry Crumble

A crumble of sharp Bramley apples and blackberries under a crunchy topping of crushed digestive biscuits, chopped hazelnuts and ground cinnamon. The apples are finely sliced and layered with the blackberries, and each layer of apples is sprinkled with light brown soft sugar, cornflour and more ground cinnamon. As it bakes, the apples cook down into a soft purée and the cornflour thickens the blackberry juices. Serve it hot, with vanilla ice cream.

| Start ahead | Prep time | Cook time | Total time | Servings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 hr | 35 min | 55 min | 1 hr 40 min | 8 |

## 📌 Before you start

- 1 hour before you start, take the unsalted butter out of the fridge, so that it's very soft (but not melted) when you make the crumble topping.

## 🔪 Equipment

- 3 large freezer bags, for crushing the digestive biscuits
- Rubber mallet
- 26 cm metal or glass pie dish, at least 5 cm deep
- Baking tray
- Foil

## 🥕 Ingredients

#### For the crumble topping

- 250 g digestive biscuits (about 17)
- 1 tsp ground cinnamon
- 100 g chopped hazelnuts
- 100 g unsalted butter (at room temperature)

#### For the filling

- 1 lemon
- 1.2 kg Bramley apples, peeled, cored and finely sliced lengthways (about 4 large)
- 150 g light brown soft sugar (used in Steps 6 and 7)
- 2½ tbsp cornflour (used in Steps 6 and 7)
- 1 tsp ground cinnamon (used in Steps 6 and 7)
- 450 g blackberries

#### To serve

- 500 ml vanilla ice cream

## 🍳 Directions

**For the crumble topping**

#### Step 1: Crush the biscuits.

Crush the biscuits by placing them inside the 3 freezer bags, one inside another, squeezing the air out of each bag before you close it. Place the bags on a concrete floor, and hit them repeatedly with the rubber mallet until the biscuits are fine crumbs.

#### Step 2: Mix the dry ingredients.

Pour the crushed biscuits into a large bowl, and mix in the ground cinnamon and chopped hazelnuts.

#### Step 3: Mix in the butter.

Mix the soft butter into the dry crumble ingredients with your fingers, until the mix is homogeneous, with no lumps of butter left.

Put an oven shelf in the middle of the oven, and preheat the oven to 180°C (160°C fan).

**For the filling**

#### Step 4: Squeeze the lemon.

Squeeze the lemon into a large bowl, making sure no seeds or pith get into the lemon juice.

#### Step 5: Prep the apples.

Peel the apples, cut them into quarters and cut out the cores. Finely slice each quarter lengthways (from the stalk end to the base), about 5 mm thick. As you go, put the slices in the bowl and toss them in the lemon juice, so they don't go brown.

#### Step 6: Toss the apples.

In a small bowl, mix 100 g of the brown sugar, 1 tbsp of the cornflour and ½ tsp of the ground cinnamon. Add it to the apples, and toss until all the apples are coated.

#### Step 7: Layer the fruit.

Put the pie dish on a baking tray. In the small bowl, mix the rest of the brown sugar (50 g), cornflour (1½ tbsp) and ground cinnamon (½ tsp), to make the sugar mix.

Place alternating layers of apples, then blackberries, then apples, and so on, in the pie dish, starting and finishing with apples. After placing each layer of apples, sprinkle an even share of the sugar mix on top of it before placing the blackberries. Aim for at least 3 apple and 2 blackberry layers.

Scrape any juice left in the apple bowl over the last layer of apples, then sprinkle the rest of the sugar mix on top.

#### Step 8: Add the crumble topping.

Sprinkle the crumble topping on top, in an even layer that reaches the edges of the pie dish, without pressing it down.

#### Step 9: Bake the crumble.

Once the oven is at temperature, put the baking tray on the oven shelf. Bake until the fruit is bubbling in the centre and the topping is golden brown, 45–55 minutes.

After 30 minutes, check the topping. If it's already golden brown, lay a sheet of foil loosely over it for the rest of the time.

> **Tip:** The fruit bubbles at the edges first. If you take the crumble out before it bubbles in the centre too, the filling will be runny.

#### Step 10: Serve the crumble.

Leave it to cool for 10 minutes, so the juices thicken, then serve it hot, with the vanilla ice cream.

## 📝 Notes and substitutions

- **Blackberries:** Frozen blackberries work too. Layer them straight from frozen, add an extra 1 tsp cornflour to the sugar mix in Step 7, and bake until the fruit is bubbling in the centre, even if that takes longer than 55 minutes.

## 🫙 Storage and make-ahead

- 🧊 **Fridge:** Up to 2 days. Leave it to cool, cover it with cling film, and refrigerate it within 1–2 hours.
- ❄️ **Freezer:** Up to 3 months in an airtight container. Thaw it in the fridge overnight, and eat it within 24 hours.
- 📅 **Make ahead:** Make the crumble topping (Steps 1–3) up to 1 week ahead, and refrigerate it in an airtight container. Break up any lumps with your fingers before Step 8.
- ♨️ **Reheat:** Reheat it in the oven at 180°C (160°C fan) for 15–20 minutes, until steaming hot all the way through.

## 🗺️ Origin

- **Country:** 🇬🇧 United Kingdom (disputed)

Crumble became popular in Britain during the Second World War, when rationing made pastry hard to make. Wikipedia gives North America as its place of origin instead, as the earliest recipes it lists come from American and Canadian magazines of 1915 and 1917.
