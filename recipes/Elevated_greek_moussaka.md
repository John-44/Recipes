# 🍆 Elevated Greek Moussaka

Layers of fried aubergines and a deep, savoury beef mince sauce, under a thick, egg-enriched béchamel with a golden crust of kefalotyri and breadcrumbs. It builds on a handwritten Greek family recipe with touches from Greek cooks: the mince is seared until deep brown, then simmered with white wine, tomatoes, a cinnamon stick, allspice and bay leaves, and the aubergines are salted before frying, so they soak up less oil. Known in Greece as *mousakás*. Serve it warm rather than hot, once the béchamel has set.

| Prep time | Cook time | Total time | Servings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 35 min | 2 hr 5 min | 3 hr 15 min | 8 |

## 📌 Before you start

- Have the ingredients ready for the [Greek Béchamel Sauce](Greek_bechamel_sauce.md), which you'll make in Step 7 (30 minutes).
- Buy 120 g kefalotyri from a Greek deli or online (60 g for this recipe and 60 g for the béchamel), or use a swap (see **Kefalotyri**).

## 🔪 Equipment

- Colander
- Pot, at least 26 cm across (not non-stick)
- Measuring cup
- Baking dish, about 20 × 30 cm and at least 7 cm deep
- Baking tray
- Probe thermometer, for reheating

## 🥕 Ingredients

- 1.25 kg aubergines (about 4)
- 2¼ tsp fine sea salt (used in Steps 1 and 4), plus extra to taste
- 1 large onion, finely chopped
- 2 garlic cloves, peeled and finely chopped
- 10 g flat-leaf parsley leaves, finely chopped
- 60 g kefalotyri, finely grated (or Pecorino Romano) (used in Steps 8 and 9)
- 1½ metric cups olive oil (used in Steps 2 and 5)
- 750 g beef mince (15% fat)
- 80 g unsalted butter (used in Steps 3 and 9)
- ¼ tsp ground allspice
- 2 tbsp tomato purée
- ½ metric cup white wine
- 2 cans (400 g each) finely chopped tomatoes
- 1 cinnamon stick
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 tsp granulated sugar, plus extra to taste
- ½ tsp freshly ground black pepper, plus extra to taste
- 1.2 L [Greek Béchamel Sauce](Greek_bechamel_sauce.md) (1 batch)
- 120 g dried breadcrumbs (used in Steps 8 and 9)

## 🍳 Directions

#### Step 1: Salt the aubergines.

Wash the aubergines, pat them dry with kitchen roll, and cut off the stalks. Slice them lengthways about 1 cm thick.

Layer the slices in the colander, sprinkling each layer with some of the salt, using 1¼ tsp in all. Leave them in the sink for at least 30 minutes.

While they salt, finely chop the onion, garlic and parsley leaves, and finely grate the kefalotyri. Then start Step 2.

> **Tip:** The salt draws water out of the aubergines, so they soak up less oil as they fry and the moussaka isn't watery.

#### Step 2: Sear the mince.

Put 1 tbsp of the oil in the pot, and heat it over high heat until it starts to smoke.

Sear the mince in 3 batches of about 250 g, by pressing each batch down gently into a flat layer in the pot. Leave it undisturbed until deep brown underneath, 2–4 minutes. Then flip it so the other side browns, about 2 minutes, then break the meat apart and tip it onto a plate. Before each of the next 2 batches, add another 1 tbsp of the oil and heat it until it starts to smoke.

If the fat smokes heavily or the fond (the browned bits stuck to the bottom of the pan) starts to blacken, turn the heat down to medium-high.

#### Step 3: Fry the aromatics.

Turn the heat down to medium and wait 1 minute, so the butter doesn't burn in the hot pot. Add 30 g of the butter, then the onion, and fry, stirring often and scraping up the fond, until the onion is soft, squidgy and slightly translucent, 8–10 minutes.

Add the garlic and allspice, and fry for 1 minute, stirring all the time. Then add the tomato purée, and fry, stirring all the time, until it turns from bright red to a darker brick colour, about 2 minutes.

#### Step 4: Simmer the meat sauce.

Turn the heat up to high and pour in the wine to deglaze the pot, scraping up the fond. Boil it until the raw wine smell has gone, 1–2 minutes.

Add the tomatoes, cinnamon stick, bay leaves, sugar, 1 tsp of the salt and the pepper. Then add the mince, with any juices from the plate, and stir well.

Bring to a simmer, then turn the heat down to medium-low, so it keeps simmering. Simmer, uncovered, stirring occasionally, until the juices have been absorbed (when you drag a spoon across the bottom of the pot, no liquid runs into the gap), 40–45 minutes. While it simmers, start Step 5, and keep stirring the sauce occasionally, more often as it thickens, so it doesn't catch on the bottom of the pot.

> **Tip:** The sauce should be much thicker than a pasta sauce. If it's runny, it soaks into the aubergines and runs out when you cut the moussaka.

#### Step 5: Fry the aubergines.

Pat the aubergines dry with kitchen roll, pressing firmly to soak up the water the salt has drawn out.

Heat 4 tbsp of the oil in a large frying pan over medium-high heat. Fry the aubergines in batches, in a single layer, until soft and lightly golden on both sides, 3–4 minutes per side, adding 2–3 tbsp more oil before each new batch. Lift them onto kitchen roll to drain.

#### Step 6: Taste and adjust.

When the sauce is ready, take the pot off the heat, lift out the cinnamon stick and bay leaves, and stir in the parsley leaves. Taste: it should be rich, savoury and tomatoey, with a gentle warmth from the spices. The kefalotyri and béchamel add salt, so season it a little less than a sauce eaten on its own.

Adjust it ¼ tsp at a time: more salt if it tastes flat, more pepper if it needs warmth, or more sugar if it tastes sharp.

#### Step 7: Make the béchamel.

Preheat the oven to 180°C (160°C fan). Make the [Greek Béchamel Sauce](Greek_bechamel_sauce.md).

#### Step 8: Layer the moussaka.

Arrange half the aubergines in neat rows in the baking dish, overlapping them slightly so they cover the bottom.

Stir half the breadcrumbs (60 g), half the kefalotyri (30 g) and 3 tbsp of the béchamel into the meat sauce, then spread it evenly over the aubergines. Arrange the rest of the aubergines on top in rows, then pour over the rest of the béchamel and spread it to the edges with the back of a spoon.

#### Step 9: Bake the moussaka.

Melt the rest of the butter (50 g) in a small saucepan over low heat. Put the rest of the kefalotyri (30 g) and breadcrumbs (60 g) in a small bowl, pour in the melted butter and toss until the crumbs are evenly covered, then sprinkle them evenly over the béchamel.

Put the baking dish on a baking tray. Once the oven is at temperature, bake until the top is golden brown, 30–40 minutes.

#### Step 10: Serve the moussaka.

Leave it to cool for 45 minutes, so the béchamel and meat sauce set. Then cut it into 8 slices, and serve it warm.

> **Tip:** Straight from the oven, the layers are too soft to cut cleanly, and the béchamel runs. As the moussaka cools, the béchamel sets firm. Many Greek cooks serve it warm rather than hot.

## 📝 Notes and substitutions

- **Kefalotyri:** A hard, salty Greek cheese made from sheep's or goat's milk, sold in Greek delis and online. If you can't find it, use the same weight of Pecorino Romano or Parmesan.
- **Home-cooked version:** For the handwritten family recipe this one builds on, with fresh tomatoes and no spices, see [Greek Home-Cooked Moussaka](Greek_home_cooked_moussaka.md).
- **Lamb instead:** Use the same weight of lamb mince, or half lamb mince and half beef mince. Lamb mince is fattier, so spoon off all but about 2 tbsp of the fat in the pot before Step 3.

## 🫙 Storage and make-ahead

- 🧊 **Fridge:** Up to 2 days. Cover the baking dish with cling film or foil, or divide the moussaka between airtight containers, and refrigerate it within 1–2 hours.
- ❄️ **Freezer:** Up to 3 months in airtight containers, or up to 1 year if vacuum packed. Thaw it in the fridge overnight, and eat it within 24 hours. The crust goes soft once it's been frozen.
- 📅 **Make ahead:** Bake it up to 2 days ahead, then leave it to cool, cover it and refrigerate it within 1–2 hours. Or make the meat sauce up to 2 days ahead: chop the onion, garlic and parsley leaves, follow Steps 2, 3, 4 and 6, then leave it to cool and refrigerate it within 1–2 hours. On the day, salt and fry the aubergines (Steps 1 and 5), reheat the sauce over medium heat, stirring often, until steaming hot all the way through, and carry on from Step 7.
- ♨️ **Reheat:** Cover it with foil and reheat it in the oven at 180°C (160°C fan) until steaming hot all the way through, about 1 hour for the whole dish. A probe thermometer pushed into the centre should read at least 75°C. Without one, a knife pushed into the centre should come out hot.

## 🗺️ Origin

- **Country:** 🇬🇷 Greece

Its name came into Greek from Arabic, through Ottoman Turkish. The Greek version, layered and topped with béchamel, was created by the French-trained Greek chef Nikolaos Tselementes in the 1920s.
