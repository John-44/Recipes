# Thai Yellow Prawn Curry

A mild, creamy curry of king prawns and potatoes in coconut milk, warm with turmeric and curry spices. It's the prawn version of *gaeng garee*, the yellow curry found on Thai restaurant menus around the world. Serve with jasmine rice.

| Prep time | Cook time | Total time | Servings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 15 min | 50 min | 1 hr 5 min, plus overnight in the fridge | 4 |

## Before you start

- Make the [Thai Yellow Curry Paste](Thai_yellow_curry_paste.md) first (35 minutes), or use store-bought paste for a quick weeknight dinner.
- If your raw king prawns are frozen, thaw them in the fridge overnight, then drain them well.
- Buy full-fat coconut milk, ideally with no emulsifiers or stabilisers, or the oil may not separate out in Step 1 (see **Coconut milk**).
- Keep the can of full-fat coconut milk in the fridge overnight, so the thick part sets firm at the top of the can and is easy to spoon off in Step 1.

## Equipment

- Wok (or a saucepan at least 24 cm across)

## Ingredients

### For the curry

- 1 can (400 ml) full-fat coconut milk (used in Steps 1, 3 and 4)
- 1 tbsp coconut oil (only if needed, see Step 1)
- 150 g [Thai Yellow Curry Paste](Thai_yellow_curry_paste.md) (or store-bought, see Notes)
- 250 ml chicken stock
- 300 g Charlotte or other waxy potatoes, peeled and cut into 2 cm chunks
- 1 small onion, cut into thick wedges
- 4 kaffir lime leaves, crumpled
- 2 tsp tamarind paste (used in Steps 4 and 6)
- 1½ tbsp fish sauce, plus extra to taste
- 1 tbsp grated palm sugar (about ¼ of a palm sugar block), plus extra to taste
- ¼ tsp MSG
- 1 small carton (250 ml) coconut cream (used in Steps 5 and 9)
- 400 g peeled raw king prawns, deveined (thawed if frozen)

### To serve

- 300 g jasmine rice
- 2 tbsp crispy fried shallots
- 10 g Thai basil or coriander leaves (optional)
- 1 red chilli, finely sliced (optional)
- Prik nam pla as an accompanying condiment (optional)

## Directions

**Step 1: Crack the coconut milk.** Without shaking the can, spoon the thick part from the top of the can (about 150 ml) into a wok or a saucepan at least 24 cm across. Bring to the boil over medium-high heat, then simmer over medium heat, stirring often, until it's very thick and the oil separates out, 7–12 minutes. If the oil hasn't separated out after 12 minutes, stir in the coconut oil.

**Step 2: Fry the prawn heads.** If you're using head-on prawns, fry the heads in the oil over medium heat, pressing them with the back of a spoon to squeeze out the rich hepatopancreas (head fat) and juices, until the oil turns orange, 2–3 minutes. Lift out the heads and any bits of hard shell.

**Step 3: Fry the paste.** Stir in the curry paste and fry it in the oil over medium heat, stirring often, until it darkens a shade, the raw shallot and garlic smell has gone and the oil separates out again, 5–8 minutes (3–5 minutes for store-bought paste). If it starts to stick, stir in 1 tbsp of the rest of the coconut milk.

> **Tip:** Under-fried paste is a common reason homemade curry tastes raw. Homemade paste is wetter than store-bought, and the wetter it is the longer it takes to fry.

**Step 4: Simmer the potatoes.** Add the rest of the coconut milk, the stock, potatoes, onion, lime leaves, fish sauce, palm sugar, MSG and half the tamarind paste (1 tsp). Bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat, then cook, uncovered, over medium-low heat until a knife slides easily into the potatoes, 15–20 minutes. While it simmers, cook the jasmine rice.

> **Tip:** Tamarind paste varies a lot in sourness from brand to brand, which is why half of it waits until Step 6.

**Step 5: Add the coconut cream.** Stir in the coconut cream, keeping back 3 tbsp for Step 9, and bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat, 2–3 minutes.

**Step 6: Taste and adjust.** Taste the sauce: it should be rich, savoury and slightly sweet, with a hint of sourness. Add more fish sauce if it needs salt, or more palm sugar if it needs sweetness. If it isn't sour enough, stir in the rest of the tamarind paste a little at a time.

**Step 7: Toast the crispy fried shallots.** Toast the crispy fried shallots in a dry frying pan over medium-low heat, stirring often, until fragrant, 2–3 minutes.

**Step 8: Cook the prawns.** Add the prawns and simmer until they turn pink and curl into a loose C, 2–3 minutes. Take the curry off the heat straight away.

> **Tip:** The prawns keep cooking in the hot sauce, and overcooked prawns go rubbery. If they curl into a tight O, they've gone too far.

**Step 9: Serve the curry.** Spoon the curry over the rice, then drizzle the rest of the coconut cream on top in a spiral. Scatter with the crispy fried shallots, then the Thai basil or coriander leaves and chilli. Optionally serve prik nam pla on the side.

## Notes and substitutions

- **Coconut milk:** For the coconut milk to separate, buy one that isn't homogenised and has no emulsifiers or stabilisers, such as guar gum or carboxymethylcellulose. [Biona Organic coconut milk](https://biona.co.uk/products/biona-organic-coconut-milk-400ml) has neither; the Thai brands Aroy-D and Chaokoh contain an emulsifier, but you can use them if you can't find one without.
- **Store-bought paste:** Use 4–5 tbsp. It's saltier than homemade, so add only half the fish sauce in Step 4, then more to taste in Step 6.
- **Buying paste:** Thai yellow curry paste is sometimes labelled "karee" or "kari" curry paste. Don't buy "yellow sour curry paste": that's for *gaeng leuang*, a different, sour and very spicy curry from Southern Thailand.
- **Tamarind paste:** Thai tamarind paste is brown and fairly runny. If yours is a thick, dark concentrate, use half the amount.
- **Prawns:** Frozen raw prawns work well. The 400 g is their weight after peeling, so if yours aren't peeled, buy about 800 g with heads and shells on (keep the heads for Step 2), or about 500 g with just the shells.
- **Chicken instead:** Use 500 g boneless, skinless chicken thighs, cut into bite-size pieces. Add them with the potatoes in Step 4, and skip Step 8.
- **More heat:** This curry is mild. For more heat, add 1 or 2 sliced bird's eye chillies with the potatoes.

## Storage and make-ahead

- **Fridge:** Up to 2 days in an airtight container. Store leftover rice separately, eat it within 24 hours, and reheat it only once.
- **Freezer:** Not recommended. The potatoes go grainy and the prawns turn rubbery.
- **Make ahead:** Make the curry up to the end of Step 4, leaving the rice until the day you serve it. Cool it, and refrigerate for up to 2 days. Reheat until simmering, then carry on from Step 5.
- **Reheat:** Take out the prawns, then reheat the curry gently on the hob until steaming hot all the way through. Stir the prawns back in and simmer for 1–2 minutes, so they don't become rubbery.

## Origin

- **Country:** Thailand

Yellow curry is closely linked to Thailand's Muslim community, and its curry powder shows an Indian influence.

## Tags

- **Course:** Mains
- **Type:** Curries
- **Main ingredient:** Seafood
- **Diet:** Dairy-free
- **Allergens:** Crustaceans, Fish. Check the labels on the chicken stock, the curry powder (or store-bought paste), the crispy fried shallots and the prawns, which can contain celery, mustard, gluten or sulphites.
