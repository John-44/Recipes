# Pumpkin Pie

The classic American Thanksgiving dessert: a smooth pumpkin filling, set with eggs and sweetened with light brown soft sugar, in a crisp shortcrust pastry case. It's warm with ground cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg and cloves, with a hint of black pepper. Cornflour helps the filling set firm enough to slice cleanly. The pastry case is blind baked for an hour, weighed down with sugar, so it stays crisp and doesn't shrink. Serve with whipped whipping cream.

| Prep time | Cook time | Total time | Servings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 30 min | 1 hr 55 min | 2 hr 35 min, plus 2 hr refrigerating and 3 hr cooling | 8 |

## Before you start

- If your all-butter shortcrust pastry is frozen, thaw it in the fridge overnight.

## Equipment

- Rolling pin
- 23 cm metal or glass pie dish, at least 4 cm deep
- Foil
- Baking tray
- Whisk
- Oven gloves, for lifting out the hot sugar
- Wire rack

## Ingredients

#### For the pastry case

- 350 g all-butter shortcrust pastry (thawed if frozen)
- 1 tsp unsalted butter
- 1 kg granulated sugar, for blind baking

#### For the filling

- 140 g light brown soft sugar
- 1 tbsp cornflour
- 2 tsp ground cinnamon
- ½ tsp ground ginger
- ½ tsp ground nutmeg (¼ tsp if freshly ground)
- ⅛ tsp ground cloves
- ½ tsp fine sea salt
- ⅛ tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 1 can (425 g) pumpkin purée
- 350 ml evaporated milk
- 3 large eggs (at room temperature)

#### To serve

- 300 ml whipping cream

## Directions

#### Step 1: Line the pie dish.

Roll out the pastry and rub a very thin layer of butter on to the sides and bottom of the pie dish.

Roll the pastry loosely around the rolling pin, then unroll it over a 23 cm metal or glass pie dish, at least 4 cm deep. Ease it into the pie dish and press it into the corners, without stretching it.

Trim the overhang to 2 cm with scissors, then fold it under so it sits on the rim of the pie dish. Crimp the edge by pressing the pastry together with fingers.

Refrigerate the pastry case for at least 2 hours, until it's cold and firm. About 20 minutes before the time is up, put an oven shelf in the lower third of the oven, and preheat the oven to 180°C (160°C fan).

> **Tip:** Rolling stretches and warms the pastry. Refrigerating the pastry case lets it relax, so it doesn't shrink in the oven, and firms it up, so the foil doesn't stick to it or push it out of shape.

#### Step 2: Blind bake the pastry case.

Put the pie dish on a baking tray. Line the cold pastry case with foil, pressing it into the corners and folding it over the crimped edge. If your foil isn't wide enough, use 2 sheets, overlapping them by 10 cm.

Fill the pastry case to the top with the granulated sugar, spreading it into the corners. Bake for 1 hour. While it bakes, take the eggs out of the fridge.

> **Tip:** The sugar settles into every corner and holds the pastry flat against the pie dish, and the moderate heat stops it shrinking or puffing up. That keeps the pastry case deep enough to hold all the filling.

#### Step 3: Make the filling.

When the pastry case has 15 minutes left, whisk the brown sugar, cornflour, spices, salt and pepper in a large bowl, breaking up any lumps. Add the pumpkin, evaporated milk and eggs, and whisk until smooth.

#### Step 4: Lift out the sugar.

Wearing oven gloves, take the baking tray out of the oven. Lift out the foil by its corners, keeping it level so the hot sugar doesn't spill, and tip the sugar into a heatproof bowl to cool.

The bottom of the pastry case should be golden brown. If it's still pale, put it back in the oven for 5 minutes more. Then turn the oven up to 200°C (180°C fan).

#### Step 5: Bake the pie.

Once the oven is hot, whisk the filling again for a few seconds, then pour it into the warm pastry case. Put the baking tray back in the oven and bake for 15 minutes.

Turn the oven down to 180°C (160°C fan), and lay strips of foil over the edge of the pastry case, so it doesn't brown too much. Bake until only the centre wobbles when you gently shake the baking tray, and the filling has puffed up slightly at the edge, about 40 minutes more.

> **Tip:** The centre keeps cooking after the pie comes out of the oven, and sets as it cools. If you wait until the centre is firm, the filling overcooks.

#### Step 6: Cool the pie.

Lift the pie dish onto a wire rack and leave it to cool for 2 hours. Then refrigerate it for at least 1 hour, so the filling finishes setting.

> **Tip:** Like any cooked food, the pie needs to go in the fridge within 2 hours, before bacteria have time to grow.

#### Step 7: Serve the pie.

Just before serving, whip the cream in a large bowl until it holds soft peaks (when you lift out the whisk, the cream forms a peak that flops over at the tip), 3–5 minutes. Cut the pie into 8 slices, and serve each one with a spoonful of the whipped cream.

## Notes and substitutions

- **Granulated sugar:** It's only there to weigh down the pastry, so it isn't eaten in the pie. Once it's cool, keep it in an airtight container and use it like any other sugar, or for blind baking again until it turns pale tan.
- **Pumpkin purée:** Look for cans of Libby's, sold in larger supermarkets and online.
- **Pie dish:** A ceramic pie dish heats up too slowly, so the pastry comes out pale and soft rather than crisp. Use a metal or glass one.

## Storage and make-ahead

- **Fridge:** Up to 2 days, covered with cling film.
- **Freezer:** Up to 3 months, wrapped tightly in cling film. Thaw it in the fridge overnight, and eat it within 24 hours.
- **Make ahead:** Make the pie up to 2 days ahead, covering it with cling film once it goes in the fridge in Step 6. Whip the cream just before serving.
