# 🍗 Thai Green Chicken Curry

A creamy, fragrant curry of chicken thighs, Thai aubergines and mangetout in coconut milk, fresh with green chillies, lemongrass and Thai basil. It's known in Thailand as *gaeng khiao waan*, or "sweet green" curry, after its pale green colour rather than its taste. This recipe adds professional Thai chef touches: cracking the coconut milk, frying the paste in its oil and seasoning with MSG. Serve with jasmine rice.

| Start ahead | Prep time | Cook time | Total time | Servings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The night before | 15 min | 50 min | 1 hr | 6 |

## 📌 Before you start

- Make the [Thai Green Curry Paste](Thai_green_curry_paste.md) first (50 minutes), or use store-bought paste.
- Buy full-fat coconut milk, ideally with no emulsifiers or stabilisers, or the oil may not separate out in Step 1 (see **Coconut milk**).
- The night before, refrigerate the can of full-fat coconut milk, so the fat rises to the top and is easy to spoon off in Step 1.

## 🔪 Equipment

- Wok (or a saucepan at least 24 cm across)

## 🥕 Ingredients

#### For the curry

- 1 can (400 ml) full-fat coconut milk (used in Steps 1, 2 and 3)
- 2 tbsp coconut oil (only if needed, see Step 1)
- 180 g [Thai Green Curry Paste](Thai_green_curry_paste.md) (or store-bought, see Notes)
- 750 g boneless, skinless chicken thighs, cut into bite-size pieces
- 200 ml low-salt chicken stock
- 6 kaffir lime leaves, crumpled
- 3 tbsp fish sauce, plus extra to taste
- 2 tbsp grated palm sugar (about ½ of a palm sugar block), plus extra to taste
- ¼ tsp MSG
- 250 g Thai aubergines, stalks removed (about 8)
- 1 small carton (250 ml) coconut cream (used in Steps 4 and 8)
- 200 g mangetout, stalks removed
- ½ lime
- 30 g Thai basil leaves

#### To serve

- 450 g jasmine rice
- 6 tbsp crispy fried shallots
- 15 g Thai basil leaves (optional)
- 1 red chilli, finely sliced (optional)
- 1 lime, cut into wedges

## 🍳 Directions

#### Step 1: Crack the coconut milk.

Without shaking the can, spoon the thick part from the top of the can (about 150 ml) into a wok or a saucepan at least 24 cm across.

Bring to boil over medium-high heat, then simmer over medium heat, stirring often, until it's thick and the oil separates out, 7–12 minutes.

If the oil hasn't separated out after 12 minutes, stir in the coconut oil.

#### Step 2: Fry the paste.

Stir in the curry paste and fry it in the oil over medium heat, stirring often, until it darkens a shade, the raw shallot and garlic smell has gone and the oil separates out again, 5–8 minutes (3–5 minutes for store-bought paste).

If it starts to stick, stir in 1 tbsp of the rest of the coconut milk.

> **Tip:** Under-fried paste is a common reason homemade curry tastes raw. Homemade paste is wetter than store-bought, and the wetter it is the longer it takes to fry.

#### Step 3: Simmer the chicken.

Stir in the chicken until it's coated in the paste. Add the rest of the coconut milk, the stock, lime leaves, fish sauce, palm sugar and MSG.

Bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat, then cook, uncovered, over medium-low heat until the chicken is no longer pink in the middle when you cut the biggest piece open, 10–15 minutes.

While it simmers, cook the jasmine rice. Cut the aubergines into bite-size pieces and put them in a bowl of cold water, so they don't turn brown.

#### Step 4: Add the coconut cream.

Stir in the coconut cream, keeping back 4 tbsp for Step 8, and bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat, 2–3 minutes.

#### Step 5: Taste and adjust.

Taste the sauce. It's eaten with plain rice, so it should be strongly seasoned: rich and savoury, with just a hint of sweetness.

Adjust it ½ tsp at a time: more fish sauce if it needs salt, or more palm sugar if it needs sweetness.

#### Step 6: Toast the crispy fried shallots.

Toast the crispy fried shallots in a dry frying pan over medium-low heat, stirring often, until fragrant, 2–3 minutes.

#### Step 7: Cook the vegetables.

Drain the aubergines and add them to the curry. Simmer over medium heat, pushing them under the sauce for the first minute so they don't turn brown, until a knife goes into them with a little resistance, about 3 minutes.

Add the mangetout and simmer until they're bright green and just tender, about 2 minutes. Take the curry off the heat, squeeze in the juice of the ½ lime, then stir in the 30 g of Thai basil until it wilts.

> **Tip:** Simmered any longer, the aubergines go soft, and the sauce darkens and loses its fresh green colour.

#### Step 8: Serve the curry.

Spoon the curry over the rice, then drizzle the rest of the coconut cream on top in a spiral. Scatter with the crispy fried shallots, then the Thai basil leaves and chilli. Serve with the lime wedges on the side.

## 📝 Notes and substitutions

- **Coconut milk:** For the coconut milk to separate, buy one that isn't homogenised and has no emulsifiers or stabilisers, such as guar gum or carboxymethylcellulose. [Biona Organic coconut milk](https://biona.co.uk/products/biona-organic-coconut-milk-400ml) has neither; the Thai brands Aroy-D and Chaokoh contain an emulsifier, but you can use them if you can't find one without.
- **Store-bought paste:** Use 100 g (about 6 tbsp). It's saltier than homemade, so add only half the fish sauce in Step 3, then more to taste in Step 5.
- **Thai aubergines:** Look for them in Asian supermarkets. They're small, round and green, crisper and more bitter than the large purple aubergines sold in UK supermarkets, and they stay slightly crunchy when cooked.
- **Thai basil:** Look for it in Asian supermarkets. If you can't find any, use the same weight of basil leaves, which taste less of aniseed.
- **More heat:** This curry is fairly mild. For more heat, add 1 or 2 sliced green bird's eye chillies with the chicken.

## 🫙 Storage and make-ahead

- 🧊 **Fridge:** Up to 2 days in an airtight container. Store leftover rice separately, eat it within 24 hours, and reheat it only once.
- ❄️ **Freezer:** Up to 3 months in a freezer bag, or up to 1 year if vacuum packed. Freeze only the curry made up to the end of Step 3, as the aubergines and mangetout go soft in the freezer. Thaw it in the fridge overnight, then finish it as in **Make ahead** and eat it within 24 hours.
- 📅 **Make ahead:** Make the curry up to the end of Step 3, but leave the aubergines whole, and the rice until the day you serve it. Cool it, and refrigerate for up to 2 days. On the day, reheat the curry until simmering, cut the aubergines as in Step 3, cook the rice, and carry on from Step 4.
- ♨️ **Reheat:** Reheat on the hob over medium-low heat until steaming hot all the way through.

## 🗺️ Origin

- **Country:** 🇹🇭 Thailand
- **Region:** Central Thailand

Green curry is a fairly recent Thai dish. It probably began in the kitchens of the Thai aristocracy in the 1920s.
