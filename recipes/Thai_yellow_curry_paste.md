# Thai Yellow Curry Paste

A mild, fragrant curry paste made from dried Thai spur chillies, lemongrass, galangal, fresh turmeric and warm spices. In Thailand it's called *prik gaeng garee*, and it's the base of Thai yellow curry. Curry powder gives it a gentle, Indian-style warmth, and fresh turmeric gives the yellow colour. This batch makes enough for 2 curries, so freeze half for next time.

| Prep time | Cook time | Total time | Makes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 30 min | 5 min | 35 min | About 250 g (enough for 2 curries for 4) |

## Before you start

- Fresh turmeric stains, so wear gloves to prepare it, and chop it with a sheet of baking paper on top of the chopping board.

## Equipment

- High-speed blender, such as a Blendtec with a Twister jar (or a stick blender and a tall, narrow jug)
- Gloves, for the fresh turmeric

## Ingredients

- 15 g dried Thai spur chillies (*prik chee fah haeng*), stalks removed (about 8 large chillies)
- 1 tbsp coriander seeds
- 1½ tsp cumin seeds
- ½ tsp white peppercorns
- 1 tbsp mild Thai curry powder
- 1 tsp coarse sea salt
- ½ tsp MSG
- 75 g Thai shallots, roughly chopped (about 8)
- 8 garlic cloves, roughly chopped
- 2 tsp Thai shrimp paste
- 2 lemongrass stalks, top half and tough outer layers removed, finely sliced (about 20 g once prepared)
- 12 g galangal, peeled and finely chopped (about a 3 cm piece)
- 12 g ginger, peeled and finely chopped (about a 3 cm piece)
- 12 g fresh turmeric, peeled and finely chopped (about a 3 cm piece)
- 10 g coriander roots, scrubbed and finely chopped (about 3)

## Directions

**Step 1: Soak the chillies.** Split the chillies open lengthways with scissors, scrape out the seeds, then snip the chillies into 2 cm pieces and put them in a heatproof bowl. Cover with boiling water and soak until soft, 15–20 minutes. Drain, keeping 3 tbsp of the soaking water.

**Step 2: Toast the spices.** While the chillies soak, toast the coriander seeds, cumin seeds and white peppercorns in a dry frying pan over medium-low heat, shaking the pan, until they're fragrant and darken a shade, 2–3 minutes. Tip them into a small bowl straight away and leave to cool for 2 minutes.

> **Tip:** The spices keep cooking in the hot pan, even off the heat, and burnt spices make the paste bitter.

**Step 3: Grind the spices.** Put the toasted spices and the curry powder in the high-speed blender and grind to a fine powder. Tip the ground spices back into the small bowl, ready for Step 5.

**Step 4: Blend the paste.** Put the 3 tbsp of soaking water, salt, MSG, chillies, shallots, garlic and shrimp paste in the high-speed blender, then add the lemongrass, galangal, ginger, fresh turmeric and coriander roots on top. Blend on the highest speed for 30 seconds, then stop and scrape down the sides. Do this 4 times. If the jar feels warm when you stop, leave it to cool for 2 minutes before blending again. Don't add extra water to help it along.

> **Tip:** Thai curry recipes expect a thick paste. Starting with the soft, wet ingredients gets the paste moving, so it blends without extra water.

> **Tip:** Long blending on the highest speed warms the paste, and heat dulls the fresh aroma of the lemongrass and galangal.

**Step 5: Blend in the spices.** Add the ground spices to the high-speed blender and blend them in the same way as in Step 4, leaving the jar to cool whenever it feels warm, until very smooth. Rub a little between your fingers: it shouldn't feel gritty.

**Step 6: Use or store.** Use half the paste for a curry that serves 4, such as Thai Yellow Prawn Curry. Freeze the rest.

## Notes and substitutions

- **Dried chillies:** Look for dried Thai spur chillies in Asian supermarkets or online. If you can't find them, use the same weight of Kashmiri chillies (sold in Indian grocers and online) or Mexican guajillo chillies, which both add colour without much heat.
- **Ground spices:** Instead of toasting and grinding whole spices, use 1 tbsp ground coriander, 1½ tsp ground cumin and ½ tsp ground white pepper.
- **Galangal:** Look for it fresh or frozen in Asian supermarkets. If you can't find it, use the same amount of extra ginger plus the zest of 1 lime.
- **Fresh turmeric:** If you can't find it, use 1 tsp ground turmeric.
- **Coriander roots:** Look for them in Asian supermarkets. If you can't find any, use the same weight of tender coriander stalks.
- **More heat:** Swap 2 or 3 of the spur chillies for small, hot dried chillies, such as bird's eye.
- **Vegetarian:** Leave out the shrimp paste, or use 2 tsp white miso instead.
- **Stick blender:** It can't grind whole spices, so use ground spices instead (see **Ground spices**). For Steps 4 and 5, put the lemongrass, galangal, ginger, fresh turmeric and coriander roots in the tall, narrow jug first, then everything else, and lift and move the stick blender around as you blend.

## Storage and make-ahead

- **Fridge:** Up to 1 week in an airtight container, with cling film pressed onto the surface so it doesn't dry out.
- **Freezer:** Up to 3 months in a freezer bag, or up to 1 year if vacuum packed. Flatten it in the bag and press a crease across the middle, so you can break off one curry's worth at a time. You can fry it straight from frozen, so there's no need to thaw it.
